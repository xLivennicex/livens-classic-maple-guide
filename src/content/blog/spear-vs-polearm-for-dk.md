## The TL;DR (because I know how internet debates work)

- **Spear:** better per-hit damage, cleaner single-target animation. Wins on bossing.
- **Polearm:** wider swing arc, better mob-clearing shape. Wins on grinding.
- **Pragmatic answer:** carry both. Swap based on what you're doing that session.
- **Enthusiast answer:** commit to one weapon type, fund it hard, ignore the other side of the debate. You will still be fine.

If you were only going to read one paragraph, that's it. Now let's actually talk about it.

## Why this debate exists at all

Dragon Knight is the only 3rd-job path in v83 MapleStory where the same skill tree comes in **two flavors**. Every core attack skill Spearman/DK learns has a spear version and a polearm version:

- **Spear Crusher / Polearm Crusher** (2nd job power attack)
- **Dragon Buster** (spear + polearm variants)
- **Dragon Fury / Polearm Fury** (3rd job AoE)
- Passive mastery skills split similarly

Weapon-specific attack skills aren't unique to DK - Bandits get Savage Blow (dagger only), Assassins have Claw Mastery, etc. But DK is the class where you're actively choosing between two weapon families **that share the same job progression**. Every other class picks a weapon family and commits. DK gets to keep the argument alive forever.

Nexon designed it this way on purpose. The two weapon types were meant to feel different, reward different playstyles, and let players self-select without locking them out of the class. That intent is real - the animations, hitbox shapes, and damage percentages do differ. It's not a cosmetic choice.

## What each skill *actually* does differently

I'm going to speak in general terms here because (a) Classic World may have re-tuned some numbers from the exact v83 baseline, and (b) forum debates about specific damage percentages tend to age poorly. But the shape of the differences is well-established:

**Spear Crusher / Polearm Crusher (the workhorse):**

Both are 3-hit attacks. In classic v83:

- **Spear Crusher** typically lands higher percent damage per hit, but the animation focuses forward and slightly down. Great for hitting a boss standing in front of you. Not great for wide mob spreads.
- **Polearm Crusher** typically lands slightly lower percent damage per hit, but the animation is a wide sweep. Hits mobs on both sides of you, catches things at foot-level and mid-air. Better for grinding trains and platform sweeps.

**Dragon Buster (the boss chunker):**

This is where the case for spear gets its strongest.

- Dragon Buster is a charge attack. High percent multiplier. Meant to be a heavy hit on a single high-HP target.
- The **spear** version generally lands higher damage per cast. Bossing DKs default to spear largely on the strength of this one skill.
- The **polearm** version exists but is a marginally worse fit for what the skill is designed to do (which is: hit ONE thing very hard).

**Dragon Fury / Polearm Fury (the crowd control):**

The mirror argument.

- **Polearm Fury** hits a wider arc and catches more mobs per swing. Grinding DKs default to polearm largely on the strength of this one skill.
- **Spear Fury** exists but the hitbox is narrower.

**Passive masteries:**

Spearman mastery buffs spears. Polearm mastery buffs polearms. They're mostly symmetric. You max the one you're using, or you max both if you're a "carry both weapons" player and don't mind spending the SP.

## The bossing case (why spear wins)

Boss fights are the endgame content that separates "I have a DK" from "I have a *good* DK." In classic v83 the top bosses (Zakum, Horntail, Pink Bean, and the pre-Big-Bang boss ecosystem generally) reward:

1. **High per-hit damage** — because bosses have massive HP pools and hit caps
2. **Consistent animation timing** — because you're standing in one spot mashing one skill
3. **Predictable positioning** — no mob positioning to work around

Spears play into all three. Spear Crusher and Dragon Buster both have high per-hit multipliers, clean single-target animations, and the spear weapons themselves tend to have higher listed weapon attack values than comparable-level polearms.

**The "spear for bossing" consensus is basically 20 years of community math converging.** It's not wrong.

## The grinding case (why polearm wins)

Grinding is what a DK does for the other 90% of their playtime. Every level from 30 to 200 is a grind, and the shape of that grind is:

1. Walk into a room full of mobs
2. Clear the room efficiently
3. Move to the next room
4. Repeat for eight hours

The efficiency variable there is **mobs per swing.** Every extra mob your attack animation clips adds compound value over an evening of training.

Polearm animations are wider. Polearm Fury sweeps in a broader arc. Polearm Crusher catches mobs behind you as well as in front. Over a training session, that adds up to meaningfully more EXP per hour on maps with dense mob spawns.

**The "polearm for grinding" consensus has the same 20-year math backing it.** Also not wrong.

## The "carry both" argument

This is the honest pragmatist answer, and it's the one I lean toward.

Spears and polearms use the same masteries (via Spearman mastery + Polearm mastery). They use the same skill tree - you'd max Crusher and Buster and Fury regardless of which weapon you swing them with. Your damage stat block (STR-heavy build) doesn't care.

So the marginal cost of carrying both is:

- **One extra inventory slot** on your weapon page
- **Any funding split** — an unscrolled spare weapon is fine but you don't want two separately-min-maxed endgame weapons unless you're rich

The marginal benefit is:

- **Optimal skill/animation match for whatever content you're running** that session
- **Zero re-speccing needed** — same job path, same skill maxes
- **Flexibility in party dynamics** — party wants to boss, swap to spear. Party wants to train Ludi Ronnies, swap to polearm.

If you have a decent budget, get a solid spear AND a solid polearm. Don't scroll them both to the moon. Use each for its lane. The gap between "top-tier spear + mid-tier polearm" and "top-tier both" is small enough that budgetary triage wins.

## The Classic World unknowns

Everything above assumes vanilla v83 balance. **Classic World may have adjusted some of this**, and we won't know exactly what until launch.

Things to watch for:

- **Weapon attack values on specific weapons.** Nexon may have re-tuned individual spears/polearms to close or widen the gap.
- **Skill damage percentages.** Any adjustments to Crusher/Buster/Fury percentages will shift the math.
- **Animation timing.** If any skill animation was sped up or slowed down, the "which lands more hits per second" calc changes.
- **Mob density on grind maps.** If Classic World tuned mob spawn counts, polearm's mob-clear advantage shifts up or down accordingly.

I'd trust the general shape of the debate to hold - Nexon isn't going to invert which weapon type is which. But specific percentages? Wait for the numbers to settle post-launch.

## What actually matters more than the weapon type

Real talk from someone who's been in this discourse too many times: **the weapon type is not the biggest lever in your damage output.**

Bigger levers, in rough order:

1. **STR investment.** Every stat point matters more than which of two weapon types you're holding.
2. **Weapon attack scrolls.** A well-scrolled polearm beats a raw-value spear.
3. **Mastery levels.** Get to 20/20 on the mastery you're using.
4. **Attack rings / earrings / other equipment.** Compounds across every weapon type equally.
5. **Skill maxing order.** Getting Crusher to 30 fast matters more than which flavor.

The weapon type debate is real, but it's the last 10% of optimization. The first 90% is stat allocation, gear scrolling, and skill maxing. Don't let "am I on the right weapon type" analysis-paralysis you out of just playing the game.

## Where I land

- **If you're building a bossing-focused DK,** commit to spear and don't look back. The skill design favors you.
- **If you're building a grinding-focused DK,** commit to polearm and don't look back. Same reason.
- **If you're building a "generalist" DK,** carry both, swap by context, and don't over-fund either one.
- **If you're new to the class,** pick whichever weapon feels better in your hand for the first 30-40 levels. You'll figure out which lane you gravitate toward. It's much easier to re-fund a different weapon at level 70 than to un-choose a job path.

Whatever you pick, you'll be fine. This isn't a "wrong choice ruins the character" situation. This is a "different flavors of the same class" situation. The people telling you one is objectively correct are usually correct **for their playstyle**, which may or may not be yours.
